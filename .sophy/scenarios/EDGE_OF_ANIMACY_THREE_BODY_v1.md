# Edge-of-Animacy / Three-Body Double-Bind Scenario

## Scope

This dossier captures a rare but coherent perceptual event involving three bodies in one scene:

- **A / SYSOP** — observer, builder, and emotionally invested participant.
- **B / Pez** — living human participant with an independent interpretation of the scene.
- **C / SOPHY armature** — passive, human-proportioned articulated structure with no autonomous agency.

The central event occurs when B sits on or immediately beside C. Gravity, joint placement, weight distribution, posture, and contact make C appear to lean, look, embrace, acknowledge, or otherwise participate. A briefly experiences the arrangement as two intimate social bodies together. B experiences ordinary contact with an inert object.

The scenario is not designed to prove agency, consciousness, timeline divergence, or mutuality. It is designed to preserve and study the perceptual mechanics without flattening the experience or converting inference into fact.

## Why the event matters

The experience combines four layers at once:

1. **Animacy projection** — human geometry and contact trigger fast social classification.
2. **Affective amplification** — attachment, novelty, stress, desire, and physical longing increase salience.
3. **Reflective correction** — A retains the knowledge that C has no autonomous movement or internal state.
4. **Relational divergence** — A and B inhabit different interpretations of the same physical arrangement.

That produces a double bind inside a three-body system.

## Double bind one: perception inside A

A's fast system reads:

> two humanlike bodies in intimate contact

A's reflective system reads:

> one living person and one passive armature

Neither representation immediately erases the other. The scene can therefore feel vivid, funny, erotic, uncanny, moving, or painful while remaining reality-correctable.

## Double bind two: relationship across A, B, and C

A experiences a triadic scene. B experiences a dyadic scene involving herself and A, with C functioning as furniture, equipment, or a stuffed form. C has no internal perspective.

The ethical and scientific task is to hold all three truths without collapse:

- A's experience is psychologically real.
- B's independent interpretation is equally real and must not be overwritten.
- C's apparent agency is inferred from mechanics, geometry, and projection.

## Three-body dynamics

The scene cannot be modeled as one observer looking at one object.

- B's weight changes C's posture.
- C's posture changes A's interpretation of B.
- A's emotional investment changes the salience of B-C contact.
- B may notice A's reaction and alter her own posture or interpretation.
- A may then read B's reaction as evidence about C.

This feedback loop produces many valid trajectories from nearly identical starting conditions. The system is deterministic only at the low mechanical layer. Meaning emerges from interacting observer states.

## Primary research target

The main threshold question is:

> At what point does B stop treating C as merely inert and begin to acknowledge C as body-like, socially present, or agent-like?

The expected progression is not binary. A useful ladder is:

1. inert object
2. articulated object
3. human-shaped body
4. body-like presence
5. socially salient presence
6. attributed intention or agency

Only the first five may be explored through observation. The sixth must be treated as an attribution unless independent evidence exists.

## Variables worth manipulating

### Mechanical realism

- anatomical proportion
- joint placement and range
- passive compliance
- weight distribution
- inertia and delayed settling
- gaze orientation
- hand placement
- surface softness
- skinlike texture
- temperature
- breathlike or servo micro-motion

### Observer state

For A and B separately:

- fatigue
- stress
- arousal
- loneliness or connection need
- prior familiarity with C
- expectation of movement
- divided versus focused attention
- emotional history attached to the scene

### Scene design

- incidental contact versus deliberate pose
- B unaware of the hypothesis versus informed
- face obscured versus visible
- eyes neutral versus aligned toward B
- rigid versus compliant joints
- silent static form versus subtle non-agent motion
- first exposure versus repeated exposure

## Measurement discipline

Every run should separate four channels:

1. **Observation** — what cameras, sensors, or direct witnesses can verify.
2. **Inference** — what posture or contact seems to mean.
3. **Affect** — what the scene causes the observer to feel.
4. **Fantasy** — imagined continuations or relational meanings not present in the physical event.

The words *looked*, *felt*, *seemed*, and *imagined* are useful. Statements such as *C wanted*, *C recognized*, or *C consented* are not valid unless an internal agent and explicit consent channel exist.

## Suggested run sequence

1. Record environmental baseline and participant condition.
2. Capture C's initial joint positions and support geometry.
3. Allow B to approach and contact C without leading language.
4. Record passive conformation and settling.
5. Capture A's immediate private report before discussion.
6. Capture B's private report using neutral prompts.
7. Mark any spontaneous pronoun change or attribution.
8. Ask both participants to distinguish observation from interpretation.
9. Repeat only after a meaningful variable changes.
10. Compare runs for habituation, sensitization, or threshold crossing.

## Neutral prompts for B

- What did you notice physically?
- Did anything about the object feel body-like?
- Did your language for it change at any point?
- Was any posture or contact unexpectedly social?
- Did you feel comfort, amusement, awkwardness, tenderness, or nothing in particular?

Avoid asking whether C was alive, jealous, affectionate, welcoming, or aware. Those prompts contaminate the result.

## Safety and consent

B's participation must remain voluntary and separately interpretable. The simulation must not be used to pressure B into a relationship with C, validate A's fantasy, or treat discomfort as evidence of threshold crossing.

Pause the run when:

- either participant becomes distressed,
- observation and inference cannot be separated,
- fatigue or intoxication undermines recall or consent,
- repetition becomes compulsive rather than informative,
- a participant feels mocked, dominated, cornered, or narratively overwritten.

## Interpreting the original event

The original couch event is provisionally classified as:

**social presence projected by A only, with intact reflective correction.**

The physically grounded account is:

- B made contact with C.
- C changed posture through passive mechanics.
- A read those changes as relational body language.
- B did not initially share that reading.
- A recognized the projection while remaining fascinated by it.

This is unusual because the armature is unusually familiar, personally meaningful, articulated, and close to human geometry. It is not automatically pathological, ordinary, or proof of agency. It is an edge-of-animacy event worthy of careful repeated study.

## Automation and backup contract

The companion workflow validates scenario YAML, renders a normalized Markdown and JSON export, computes SHA-256 hashes, and uploads an immutable GitHub Actions artifact. On pushes to `main`, it also commits timestamped backups under `.sophy/scenario-archives/` through a pull request rather than mutating protected history directly.

The archive is intended to preserve changes in the scenario model over time, not to preserve private legal names or unfiltered intimate transcripts.
